# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue about code, not about each other. Assume the person on the other
side is trying to make something good.

## What is expected

- Give feedback on the work, with reasons. "This breaks progress tracking because IDs move"
  is useful. "This is terrible" is not.
- Accept that maintainers may decline a contribution. The project has firm constraints, and
  a "no" is about scope, not about you.
- Be patient. This is a small project maintained around other work, so replies can take
  days.
- Respect that people here are working across languages. English is the working language of
  the repository, and imperfect English is welcome. So is a bug report in Japanese.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in issues, pull requests, commit messages,
  reviews, or the Discord.
- Demeaning comments about anyone's nationality, language ability, gender, sexuality, race,
  religion, disability, age, appearance, or experience level.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their consent.
- Sustained disruption: repeatedly reopening settled decisions, brigading a thread, or
  demanding unpaid work as though it were owed to you.

## Scope

This applies in every project space, including the GitHub repository, issues, pull requests,
discussions, and the project Discord, and it applies when someone is representing the
project in public.

## Reporting

Email **alok@asakiri.com** with what happened, where, and links if you have them. Reports go
to the maintainer only and are not shared further without your agreement, unless there is a
credible risk to someone's safety.

You will get an acknowledgement within about a week. If your report concerns the maintainer,
say so, and you may ask a third party you trust to relay it on your behalf.

## Enforcement

Responses are proportionate, and usually start at the low end:

1. A private note explaining what was wrong.
2. A public correction on the thread.
3. A temporary block from project spaces.
4. A permanent ban.

Deliberate harassment or any threat to someone's safety skips straight to the last step.
